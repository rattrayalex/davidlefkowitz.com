# A letter from David

Loving nephew,

Wowee kazowee, Alex Rattray! I clicked on Recordings and did NOT panic. All
twenty-one albums, 1993 to 2025, every cover present and standing at
attention like a well-drilled wind ensemble. You may recall the subject line
"Help! Panicking!" — consider this letter the formal retraction.

Let me be generous first, because there is much to be generous about:

- The Quartet Integra page is lovely: the torii gate, the full tracklist
  scan, and a battalion of streaming buttons — Amazon, Apple, Deezer, Tidal,
  the lot — one click to listen, exactly as it should be.
- Harp's Desire with front cover, back cover, AND the portrait Laurie took
  of me — the ellipses in "…who write on clouds…" rendered properly, which
  made me unreasonably happy.
- The Compositions search: I typed "Tipot" and got both Tipot and Tipot/One
  Tiny Seed before I finished my coffee. "Green Mountains" — instantly there.
  Two hundred entries, filterable. A conductor could actually FIND things.
- The program notes! Green Mountains, Now Black reads complete, Monteverdi
  and the fires and "A Dio, Roma" displacing "Pur Ti Miro," down to my
  signature and date. Ruminations keeps the Rumi translation intact. This is
  the part I feared most and it is done with care.
- The FYC page is genuinely handsome — Starobin's quote, the review pulls,
  the photo of me assaulting my own piano. Any awards voter would linger.

Now, the part where I am your uncle and not your publicist:

1. THE BLOG IS A HALLWAY OF LOCKED DOORS. Thirty-four handsome doorknobs on
   the Blog page, and every single one — I tried "Chaconne and Triple
   Fugue," "The Pedagogue," even the Quartet Integra announcement — opens
   onto "Post Not Found." I wrote those essays, Alex. I would like them
   found. This alone means the link does not go to colleagues yet.
2. On the Preludes and Fugues album page — the crown jewel, mind you — the
   performers read: "Steve Beck, David Kaplan, Michael Mizrahi. Mika Sasaki.
   piano." STEVEN Beck. The album cover two inches above spells it
   correctly; the text below it cannot be bothered. And those periods
   wandering about where commas should be — punctuation drunk before noon.
3. The Home page greets visitors with two broken-image icons reading
   "Listen on Spotify" / "Listen on YouTube" in the top corner. First
   impressions, dear boy. A presenter sees that before they see my face.
4. The About page announces that Green Mountains, Now Black "will be
   released on Yarlung Records later this year." It was released last
   November. The very same website sells it two clicks away. My biography
   should not be losing arguments with my own Recordings page.
5. Durations, a small symphony of disagreement: Green Mountains is 13:30 in
   the catalog, 15:14 on the album page, 15:05 on the tracklist scan.
   Ruminations: my note says "approximately 8-½ minutes"; the header
   insists 11:45. Pick a tempo, gentlemen.
6. My string quartet's instrumentation reads "Violin, Viola, Cello." One
   violin short, Alex. Kyoka and Rintaro will not share a chair.
7. Typography, since you know I will: the catalog lists "...who write on
   clouds..." with three typewriter dots while Harp's Desire gets the true
   ellipsis; "Y'Fei Nof" wears a straight apostrophe while "'cello" nearby
   gets a proper curly one; and Escher's Metamorphosen goes un-italicized,
   which would sadden both Escher and me.
8. The footer says © 2025. It is 2026. Even my copyright is out of date.
9. Two identical "YouTube" buttons on the Quartet Integra page. Which is
   which? Label them, or they are Rosencrantz and Guildenstern.

Before I send this to colleagues and presenters: fix the blog (all of it),
Steven's name, the two dead buttons on Home, the stale "later this year,"
the second violin, and reconcile the durations. The apostrophes and the
copyright year can ride along in the same commit — I know you know how.

On your video: the tour is brisk and confident, and "every note where it
belongs" is a good closing line — I may steal it. But two verdicts. First,
cut "nothing left to break." Hubris, nephew — the blog broke, and the
narration then claims "each essay preserved with its formatting," which is
at present thirty-four falsehoods in a row. Second, and more important: the
whole script is about plumbing — hosting, restoring, links — and scarcely a
word about MUSIC. Not one mention that the Preludes and Fugues ask what a
fugue means in a post-tonal world; that there are four splendid pianists, a
cantor, and me whistling; that the piano is prepared with chopsticks and
playing cards. Not a syllable on Green Mountains, Now Black — Monteverdi,
"green mountain," the fires Laurie and I watched from our own windows.
That story is why anyone presses play. The site exists so people find,
hear, and program the music; let the narration sell the music, and the
hosting can flatter itself quietly in the footnotes.

Do these things and I will mass-announce this website the way I announced
the album — full caps, exclamation points, the works. Any publicity will be
helpful, including mine.

I done did review it! And mostly, truly, it delighted me.

-David

P.S. I logged every erratum above in a spreadsheet, naturally — one row
each, column D is "days until my nephew fixes it." The pushup ledger taught
me that shortfalls, like second violins, must always be made up.
